Shipment 1 in one paragraph

What you would be able to do

One r640-hypervisor.efi, signed, booted from iDRAC virtual media or USB on a PowerEdge R640. You reach an HTTPS page with your own certificate and your own account, on a management port with a static address you chose. You upload an Ubuntu ISO, create a guest with the CPUs, RAM and disk you want on the PERC array you already own, attach a NIC, and the page shows you that the guest uplink has link before you press Start. The guest installs, reboots to its disk, gets an address on your network, and you SSH to it from your desk. You can type into it from the browser when SSH is not an option. When the rack loses power the guest comes back on its own. When a new RayNu-V ships you update from the page and roll back from the page. Every one of those actions is in an audit log you can export. Nothing in that paragraph requires a serial console.

Today's lab box already does part of that paragraph: the install loop, Ubuntu to login:, the PERC array, persist across power-off, HTTPS, a guest NIC that transmits. The rows below say which part, and what is still only a sentence here.

How to read a row

Three words, one rule

  • lived — printed on the PowerEdge R640 (or, for a host artifact such as a proof or a size gate, closed in CI), with a commit SHA or an iron marker in the note. A laptop or QEMU does not make a row lived. The sync tool rejects a lived row without evidence.
  • in flight — code exists on a branch or pull request and is under iron test. The note names it.
  • planned — accepted in ADR-021, not built. No evidence, no claim.
  • Rows move by editing the ADR with evidence, reviewed like code. Demotions are allowed and are recorded in the HDA changelog. Counts here are not a score; the distance tracker is Everest, which stays at 99% until the M8 gaps shrink.
