The pressure most CIOs are under right now

Broadcom’s changes to the VMware estate forced a wave of re-evaluation. License economics shifted. Support and upgrade paths tightened. Many organizations are being pushed toward hardware refresh cycles they did not plan — not because the servers failed, but because staying “current and patchable” on the incumbent stack became expensive or constrained.

At the same time, the alternatives on the table often trade one problem for another: softer isolation guarantees, larger attack surfaces, host-OS sprawl, or operational models that your teams do not yet trust under audit.

The rational question is no longer only “which hypervisor.” It is: how do we keep productive Dell iron working for years longer without accepting weaker security or walking deeper into a licensing treadmill?

What RayNu-V changes

RayNu-V is a single-binary Type-1 bare-metal hypervisor written in Rust and optimized for Dell PowerEdge R640, R650, and R660. There is no host operating system, no package sprawl, and no configuration surface that grows with every release.

Why formal verification matters to the CIO

Most hypervisors ask you to trust testing, process, and historical track record. That is necessary — and incomplete. RayNu-V treats the isolation boundary as something that must be proved.

The core claim is simple and strong:

For every valid guest-to-host memory mapping, that physical frame is owned by exactly one guest — and belongs to neither the hypervisor nor any other guest.

That property is pursued with Verus and Kani on a deliberately small Proven Core. Maturity is published honestly in a living verification paper. Claims never run ahead of evidence. For regulated, sovereign, or high-assurance environments, this is the difference between “we tested a lot” and “here is the machine-checked argument.”

What this means in practical terms

  • You are not forced into a premature refresh Productive R640-class servers can remain in service while you evaluate a path that does not lock you into the next licensing cycle.
  • Security posture improves instead of softening Moving off a mature incumbent does not have to mean accepting a weaker isolation story. Formal methods are the wedge.
  • Operational familiarity without the lock-in Teams that understand vSphere-style operational loops get a clear, single-binary control surface on Dell iron they already know how to manage via iDRAC.
  • Evidence that survives scrutiny Design partners and early evaluators can inspect the living paper, the audit trail, and the iron evidence — not a deck that ages out of date the week after the meeting.

Honest status

RayNu-V is early and evidence-first. Lab gates through M6 are closed on Latitude + QEMU. Real PowerEdge R640 has closed boot and UEFI-HTTP bring-up markers. The living verification paper tracks what is actually proved versus what remains open. We do not claim a finished product. We claim a clear architecture, a verified-core north star, and measurable progress on the iron you care about.

That honesty is deliberate. CIOs who have lived through over-promised migrations prefer a path they can inspect over a narrative they have to take on faith.
