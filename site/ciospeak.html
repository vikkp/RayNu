The pressure many CIOs are under

Broadcom’s ownership of VMware changed the economics. License costs rose. Support and upgrade paths tightened. Organizations that planned multi-year life for their PowerEdge fleets now face pressure to refresh hardware — not because the servers failed, but because staying current and supported on the incumbent stack became more expensive or constrained.

The alternatives on the table often trade one concern for another: softer isolation guarantees, larger attack surfaces, host-OS sprawl, or operational models that security and audit teams are still evaluating.

The practical question many leaders are asking is: how do we keep productive Dell iron working for years longer without accepting weaker security or walking deeper into a licensing treadmill?

What RayNu-V changes for the business

RayNu-V is a single-binary Type-1 bare-metal hypervisor written in Rust and optimized for Dell PowerEdge R640, R650, and R660. There is no host operating system, no package sprawl, and no configuration surface that grows with every release. The security-critical path is designed from day one for formal verification.

Why formal verification matters — and where we stand

Most hypervisors ask you to trust testing, process, and historical track record. That is necessary — and incomplete. RayNu-V treats the isolation boundary as something that should ultimately be proved.

The core property we are working toward is deliberately simple and strong:

For every valid guest-to-host memory mapping, that physical frame is owned by exactly one guest — and belongs to neither the hypervisor nor any other guest.

Full formal verification of that property will take time. We do not claim it is finished. What we do claim is that RayNu-V is designed from the first line for machine-checked isolation, publishes a living verification paper that tracks what is actually proved versus what remains open, and is already closer to that standard than any commercial hypervisor currently running on the same PowerEdge R640-class iron.

Maturity is published honestly. Claims never run ahead of evidence. For regulated, sovereign, or high-assurance environments, the difference between “we tested a lot” and “here is the machine-checked argument we are building” is material — even while the work is still in progress.

What this means in practical terms

  • You are not forced into a premature refresh Productive R640-class (and later R650/R660) servers can remain in service while you evaluate a path that does not lock you into the next licensing cycle.
  • A stronger isolation trajectory, not a weaker one Leaving a mature incumbent does not require accepting a softer isolation story. The formal-methods foundation is the differentiator — even while the full proofs are still maturing.
  • Operational familiarity without the lock-in Teams that understand vSphere-style operational loops get a clear, single-binary control surface on Dell iron they already know how to manage via iDRAC.
  • Evidence that survives scrutiny Design partners and early evaluators can inspect the living paper, the audit trail, and the iron evidence — not a deck that ages out of date the week after the meeting.

Honest status — by design

RayNu-V is early and evidence-first. Lab gates through M6 are closed on Latitude + QEMU. Real PowerEdge R640 has closed boot and UEFI-HTTP bring-up markers. The living verification paper tracks what is actually proved versus what remains open. We do not claim a finished product. We claim a clear architecture, a verified-core north star, and measurable progress on the iron you care about.

That honesty is deliberate. Leaders who have lived through over-promised migrations prefer a path they can inspect over a narrative they have to take on faith. Transparency is part of the product.
